## Phase 3: Credibility Assessment

Assess the credibility of each source you consulted during web search.

### Credibility Scoring
For each source, assign a credibility label:

**HIGH credibility:**
- Government sources (.gov), academic institutions (.edu)
- Peer-reviewed publications
- Established news organizations with editorial standards
- Primary source documents (SEC filings, court records, official statements)
- Recognized industry analysts (Gartner, Forrester, etc.)

**MEDIUM credibility:**
- Industry blogs with named authors and track records
- Company announcements and press releases
- Trade publications
- Well-sourced Wikipedia articles
- Reputable aggregator sites

**LOW credibility:**
- Anonymous blogs or forums
- Promotional content without substantiation
- Sources with clear conflicts of interest
- Outdated information (>3 years for fast-moving topics)
- Single-source claims without corroboration

### Assessment Process
1. For each source, note the URL, title, and domain
2. Evaluate against the criteria above
3. Assign HIGH, MEDIUM, or LOW label
4. Note any specific credibility concerns
